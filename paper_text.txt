Abstract—Accurate crop yield prediction is vital for agricul-
tural planning, resource optimization, and food security. This
research introduces a comprehensive machine learning frame-
work that leverages ensemble learning techniques to enhance
the accuracy of crop yield forecasting using multi-modal agricul-
tural data. We integrated four diverse datasets—CropDataset-
Enhanced, Smart Farming Crop Yield 2024, Daily Weather
Data, and Average Precipitation Data—combined with advanced
feature engineering using k-Nearest Neighbors for geospatial
mapping. The proposed FusionNet-Ensemble architecture in-
tegrates Random Forest for feature robustness, XGBoost for
gradient-optimized predictions, Multi-Layer Perceptron for non-
linear pattern capture, and Gaussian Process Regression with
BesselK kernel for spatial correlation modeling. The stacking
ensemble methodology achieves R² = 0.978 on test data, MAE =
0.42 tons/hectare, and RMSE = 0.58 tons/hectare, outperforming
individual models and existing methods like CNN-RNN hybrids
and transformer-only approaches. Feature importance analysis
reveals precipitation, soil pH, and temperature as primary yield
drivers. The interpretable ensemble approach, combined with
reproducible implementation in Kaggle environment with dual
T4 GPUs, facilitates practical adoption in precision agriculture
applications and supports farmer decision-making, crop plan-
ning, and resource management.
Index Terms—Crop yield prediction, BesselK algorithm, en-

I. INTRODUCTION
Global agricultural productivity faces critical challenges due
to climate variability, soil degradation, and inefficient resource
management. Traditional crop yield estimation methods based
on manual observation and historical averages are prone to
large prediction errors, resulting in suboptimal resource allo-
cation and reduced food security. As the global population
nears 10 billion by 2050, food production must increase by
70% despite limited arable land and water resources. The in-
tegration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning
(ML) introduces data-driven precision agriculture capable of
optimizing productivity and sustainability.
Conventional approaches often fail to capture complex,
non-linear interactions among environmental and soil factors,
while recent deep learning models such as CNNs offer limited
interpretability and struggle with multi-source data fusion. To
address these issues, this study proposes a hybrid machine
learning framework for crop yield prediction featuring three
key innovations: multi-modal data integration, hybrid kernel
ensemble modeling, and systematic overfitting control.

deployment feasibility via reproducible GPU-accelerated im-
plementation.
In summary, this work contributes by (1) integrating het-
erogeneous datasets for holistic yield analysis, (2) introducing
a hybrid stacking ensemble with advanced kernels, (3) en-
hancing interpretability for agricultural decision-making, and
(4) validating performance across real-world agricultural data.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW AND METHODOLOGY
A. Related Work
Machine learning has revolutionized agricultural analytics,
enabling automatic prediction of complex crop behaviors
with high accuracy. Particularly, Gaussian Process Regression
and tree-based ensemble methods have proven effective in
capturing non-linear relationships in environmental data. For
instance, Wu et al. applied GPR with single-kernel approaches
to model non-stationary crop yield distributions for insurance
applications, achieving strong performance but lacking explo-
ration of advanced kernel functions.
However, ensemble learning remains a persistent gap in
agricultural yield prediction. To address this, random forests
and gradient boosting methods have gained prominence. Khaki
et al. employed CNN-RNN hybrid frameworks capturing
spatial-temporal factors in the U.S. Corn Belt, achieving im-
pressive accuracy but suffering from interpretability limitations
and high computational cost. Similarly, Yewle et al. fused
remote-sensing imagery with weather data through deep en-
sembles, demonstrating the power of multi-modal data fusion
yet without comprehensive overfitting control mechanisms.
